Attendance Allowance is paid at one of two weekly rates in 2026/27: £76.70 or £114.60. The rate depends on whether someone needs help during the day only, or across both day and night. Savings and income have no bearing on the amount.

## The two weekly rates for 2026/27

- **Higher rate: £114.60 a week.** This is for help or supervision across both day and night. It also applies where a medical professional has said someone is nearing the end of life.
- **Lower rate: £76.70 a week.** This is for frequent help or constant supervision during the day, or supervision at night.

The difference between the two is £37.90 a week. Need at night is the deciding factor. Neither the person's diagnosis nor the amount of care they already receive is the test.

## April 2026 increase

Both rates rose that month.

- The lower rate went from £73.90 to £76.70, which is £2.80 more a week.
- The higher rate went from £110.40 to £114.60, which is £4.20 more a week.

Across a full year, that comes to £145.60 and £218.40 extra respectively. These annual figures are our own calculation from the weekly rates.

## Timing of rate changes

Rates are reviewed each April, and the new figures are usually announced months beforehand. For 2026/27, a written ministerial statement came on 26 November 2025, and the rates were confirmed on 16 February 2026.

A figure with no date beside it could be last year's, so the tax year is worth checking on anything read, including this article.

## Savings and income

Attendance Allowance is not means-tested. GOV.UK states that what someone earns, or how much they have in savings, will not affect what they get. There is no capital limit and no income test. A household with savings and a private pension could still qualify for the higher rate, which comes to £5,959.20 over a year (our calculation).

## Payment frequency

GOV.UK says Attendance Allowance is usually paid every 4 weeks. The money goes into a bank, building society or credit union account.

## People who are excluded

The weekly rates do not apply to everyone. GOV.UK says Attendance Allowance cannot be claimed by anyone who gets:

- Disability Living Allowance
- Personal Independence Payment
- Adult Disability Payment
- Scottish Adult Disability Living Allowance
- Armed Forces Independence Payment

Care homes are a separate case. Someone usually cannot get it if they live in a care home and their local authority pays for their care. They can still claim if they pay all their care home costs themselves.

Anyone who also receives Constant Attendance Allowance will see their Attendance Allowance reduced by the amount of Constant Attendance Allowance they get.

## Effect on other benefits

The weekly figure is not always the whole picture. GOV.UK states that someone who receives Attendance Allowance could get extra Pension Credit, Housing Benefit or Council Tax Reduction. It suggests checking with the helpline or office that deals with the benefit in question. The pages checked describe no case where getting Attendance Allowance reduces Pension Credit.

## Scotland

People living in Scotland do not claim Attendance Allowance. They apply for Pension Age Disability Payment instead, run by Social Security Scotland. Its rates are published separately, so the figures above describe a benefit that people in Scotland do not claim.

## What the claim form asks

Knowing the two rates does not tell anyone which one they would be paid, or whether they would be paid at all. That turns on the help needed on the worst days rather than the best. The night questions on the claim form ask how often help is needed each night and for how many minutes each time. They cover things such as turning over in bed, getting to and using the toilet, and being propped up.
